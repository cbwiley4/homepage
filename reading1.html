Reading Responses



"My website is a shifting house next to a river of knowledge. What could yours be?" by Lauren Schwulst

I like the idea that clarity isn’t the only possible intention for the website. You could make the website purposefully confusing and unpredictable. “They are both subject and object at once.” A website can look and behave however you want it to. In today’s age, the internet is controlled by corporations. These sites are geared towards monetization instead of user experience. Artists can translate their world-building skills into making websites. Having a website for an artist’s own work is beneficial because it allows you to control how your work is viewed. It is like being your own gallery organizer. I like the idea of a website being a finite room. It can often feel overwhelming being on the web and having so many things be interconnected. Websites don’t have to be complete when first published. It can grow as the needs grow.

"A Rant about Technology" by Ursula K. Le Guin

This is a good reminder of how technology has become a misused term. Things like medicine, pens, paper, chairs, are all technology, no matter how mundane. We can respect simple technology. It goes unnoticed how it simplifies everyday tasks. When we change how we view the term “technology”, it becomes less daunting to think about working with and developing our own. Technology is not exclusive to things we consider hi tech.

"Against an Increasingly User-Hostile Web" by Parimal Satyal

I agree with the author that the internet is made to exploit its users. I notice when talking to family members about political news on social media, each is getting fed different information. I appreciated the list at the end on how both users and developers can protect themselves online. It feels inevitable to have so much data collected with each interaction of the internet. I am not particularly worried about a personalized feed of information for myself. I am aware of everything having an agenda and like to cross check sources for everything. It does worry me in general though, especially for older generations, who take everything at face value. I feel like a lot of harmful ideologies are encouraged by the internet and what it pushes to its users.

"The Good Room. Spaces speak. Digital ones should, too." by Frank Chimero

He is right that the last decade of technology has turned into a place where we live. I still remember when I was little having technology be new and exciting. It felt like an addition to life, instead of a part of life itself. I love the library. When I go to spend time there, they don’t push anything on me except more materials to explore. The internet often feels like it takes knowledge away. Most of the spaces we occupy every day are within technology. And yet I don’t think people give it as much of a thought as the physical spaces around them. I think earlier technology felt like using the technology INSIDE the place, but now it is the place itself. “Our minds can be gruesomely specific about our fears, but can only make vague, imprecise gestures at our desires through broad words like love, happiness, or contentedness.”I liked this quote in general. I think this applies to everything. I am scared of spiders and I desire to be happy.
